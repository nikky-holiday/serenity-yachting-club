Yachting for beginners: 7 tips before your first sea voyage

Category: Guides Author: Serenity Team Published: 15 July 2026 7 min read

Yachting can seem complicated, too sporty or reserved for the "chosen few" who have known how to read the wind and tie sailors' knots since childhood. Images of storms, heeling decks and strict discipline come to mind.

In reality, a modern voyage on a sailing catamaran is far closer to a relaxed stay in a floating boutique hotel, where the horizon and the view from your window change every morning. It's a chance to escape the noise of the city, switch off your phone notifications and return to the natural rhythm of life.

If you dream of the sea but still hesitate — here are seven things worth knowing so you can step aboard with confidence and enjoy this holiday to the full.

1. No experience needed. The captain takes care of everything

The main myth among beginners is the belief that you'll have to work as a deckhand on board. That's not the case.

Every one of our catamarans has a professional captain. This is someone who not only knows the waters, the wind forecasts and the quietest deserted coves inside out, but who also takes full responsibility for your safety, the mooring and coordinating the route.

You don't need to know how to raise the sails, plot a course on charts or understand nautical terms. Your only job is to relax. That said, if you're curious about seamanship, the captain will happily give you a briefing, teach you to hold the wheel and feel the wind.

In short: you're going to rest, not to sit an exam. All the technical handling of the vessel is on the crew.

2. A catamaran means stability, space and thought-through comfort

If you worry about seasickness, forget the stereotypes about single-hull yachts. A catamaran is, in essence, a sailing villa on two parallel floating platforms. Thanks to this design it sits confidently on the water, doesn't heel in the wind and barely rocks on the waves.

Life on board is arranged to be as comfortable and straightforward as possible:

  • Cabins: private rooms with full-size double beds, fresh linen and en-suite bathrooms (a shower with hot water, a toilet, a mirror).
  • Power and climate: the boat has its own generator, air conditioning and 220V sockets to charge your phones and cameras at any time.
  • Space: a spacious saloon, a cosy cockpit with a large table for shared breakfasts and the legendary net on the bow of the catamaran, where you can lie for hours above the turquoise water.

3. Packing your bag: the philosophy of a soft wardrobe

The main rule of preparing for the voyage is gentle minimalism. Space is precious on a catamaran, so the first thing to forget is a big hard-shell suitcase.

What's worth bringing:

  1. A soft bag or backpack. You'll lay your things out on the shelves and in the wardrobes in your cabin, then fold the soft bag flat and tuck it away in a niche.
  2. Linen shirts and swimwear. You'll spend most of the time in the sun. Loose long-sleeved linen protects from UV better than any cream and looks effortlessly elegant.
  3. A cosy evening layer. Evenings in sea coves bring a pleasant cool. Bring one soft oversized hoodie, sweatshirt or blanket-cardigan for romantic evenings under the stars.
  4. Beauty gadgets. There are no fixed hairdryers or irons on a catamaran. You're welcome to bring your favourite hairdryer, and instead of a heavy iron a compact portable steamer is better — it quickly freshens linen right in your cabin.
  5. Footwear. On deck we go barefoot — an incredibly pleasant feeling of freedom. If you do wear shoes, choose moccasins or trainers with a light sole. Comfortable sandals will be handy for going ashore.

4. A day on board is a rhythm, not a strict schedule

In ordinary life we're always rushing somewhere. On a catamaran, time obeys quite different laws — the laws of the calm sea (Slow Living).

A typical day on board has nothing in common with classic sightseeing tours:

  • Morning: you wake to the soft splash of the waves, step out on deck and sip fragrant coffee right in a deserted cove, then dive off the stern into mirror-clear water.
  • Day: a short, comfortable crossing under sail (2–3 hours) while you sunbathe or read a book. A stop by a scenic grotto, lunch from fresh local produce, some SUP.
  • Evening: a walk ashore on an old island, dinner in a family taverna and a return to the catamaran to watch the stars.

The route is flexible. If you fall in love with some secret cove — we can linger there for a few more hours, because this is your voyage.

5. An intimate atmosphere and the people beside you

You'll spend a week on one vessel, so the company matters enormously. At Serenity Yachting Club we take great care in forming the groups.

We gather small companies (6 to 12 people) — always thoughtful, interesting and empathetic travellers who value a fine holiday, aesthetics and personal boundaries. Very often people who meet on board on the first day go home as close friends.

And if you crave absolute privacy — we can always arrange a private charter of the vessel just for your family or closest circle.

The sea doesn't like haste. The best memories are born not where you rushed to, but where you allowed yourself to stop and simply breathe.

6. A transparent budget: no hidden surprises

For a truly relaxed holiday, the financial side should be completely clear while you're still on shore.

What's already included in the cabin price:

  • accommodation on a comfortable catamaran;
  • the services of a professional captain;
  • full preparation of the vessel, fresh-water refill, a set of fresh linen and towels;
  • auxiliary equipment (SUP boards, snorkelling gear).

What's planned separately: flights, personal transfers and the "ship's kitty" — the group's shared, transparent budget for fuel, marina stops and provisioning on board. We always calculate this estimate in detail in advance, so that on the voyage you only think about which wine to have with dinner.

7. Choose a direction that matches your pace

The key to a perfect first voyage is the right choice of waters, one that matches your inner wish:

  • Cosy classic (Greece / Saronic Gulf): perfect for a first encounter. A calm sea without waves, short crossings between islands, cosy fishing villages, ancient sites and delicious street food.
  • Vivid hedonism (Italy / Lipari Islands): dramatic volcanic landscapes, wine tasting on the island of Salina, swimming in black-sand coves and dinner on deck under the flashes of the Stromboli volcano.
  • Deep restoration (Wellness retreat on the Liparis): a blend of relaxed yachting, sunrise yoga practices, a light healthy menu and a full mental reset.

A first voyage under sail almost always turns into not a one-off adventure but a new habit that changes the quality of your life.
