Charter

Chartering yachts and catamarans worldwide: from the idea to your first sunrise at sea — without stress or bureaucracy

Category: Charter · Guides Author: Serenity Team Published: 1 August 2026 9 min read

A sea voyage on a private yacht or catamaran is one of the highest forms of freedom in modern travel. It's the chance to wake up each morning in a new quiet cove with crystal water, to see corners of the planet hidden from ordinary tourists, and to spend time with your closest people — away from hotel noise and crowds.

But behind every flawless social-media shot lies a significant amount of organisational work. Finding the right vessel, the technical survey, the fine print of international contracts, security deposits, logistics and communication with foreign charter companies — all of it takes time and experience.

For those planning a first cruise — and even for experienced captains — this routine often becomes a source of headaches. At Serenity Yachting Club we believe a holiday should stay a holiday, from the very first minute of planning.

Below is how the charter process around the world actually works, what pitfalls await travellers, and how we take the entire organisational burden off your shoulders.

Stage 1. The waters and the type of vessel: yacht or catamaran?

The first question for anyone who wants to put to sea: where to go and on what? This choice determines not only the budget but the whole character of your voyage.

Geography and seasonality

The world of yachting lives by clear seasons, so it's important to match the destination to the time of year:

  • May — October (the Mediterranean). The gold standard: the Ionian Islands of Greece (Corfu, Lefkada, Zakynthos), the cove-lined coast of Croatia, refined Italy (Amalfi, Sardinia, Sicily) or the French Riviera. A calm sea, exquisite cuisine and rich history.
  • November — April (exotic waters). When Europe turns cold, the season moves to the tropics: the Seychelles with their granite rocks and turtles, the Caribbean (the Bahamas, the British Virgin Islands) or Thailand. A time for those who dream of eternal summer.

Monohull or catamaran

Two different philosophies of holiday:

  1. A classic sailing yacht (monohull). The choice for lovers of romance, dynamics and classic aesthetics. It cuts through the wave beautifully and gives a sense of thrill as it heels under sail, but calls for some flexibility because of its more compact cabins.
  2. A sailing or motor catamaran. In essence, a modern premium-class floating villa. Thanks to its two hulls, a catamaran has high stability: it barely heels in the wind and rocks far less on the waves. You get a spacious saloon, a large cockpit for open-air meals, separate wide cabins with en-suite bathrooms, and the famous net on the bow for sunbathing.
You don't need to spend days on sailing forums and reviews. You describe your wishes — the company, whether there are children, the desired comfort level, the budget — and we put together a personal shortlist of the 3–4 best vessels for your request.

Stage 2. Finding the vessel and the technical survey

At first glance chartering a yacht is like renting a car, but the safety and technical requirements here are far higher. There are thousands of charter companies worldwide, from large international networks to small private owners, and far from all of them maintain their fleet to the same standard.

Beautiful catalogue photos may have been taken when the boat was launched from the shipyard a few years ago. In practice an inexperienced charterer risks unpleasant surprises:

  • a faulty watermaker or generator;
  • worn sails or outdated navigation equipment;
  • an unpleasant smell in the air-conditioning or plumbing;
  • worn batteries that leave the fridges dead at anchor.
We work exclusively with proven global operators and have our own quality-assessment system — a checklist of over 40 points. We guarantee the technical soundness, cleanliness and full outfitting of every vessel. You get exactly the boat you chose from the photos and specifications.

Stage 3. Legal clarity, contracts and deposits

A charter party is a complex international document, usually in a foreign language. A person without experience easily misses nuances that later turn into financial losses.

Where difficulties usually arise

  • Security deposit and excess. When taking over the boat, a significant sum is frozen on your card (from €2,000 to €5,000). If at the end of the cruise the company finds even a small scratch on the gelcoat or minor damage, you risk losing this money.
  • Hidden charges. The base price often excludes "mandatory extras": final cleaning, the transit log, hire of the dinghy's outboard motor, bed linen or tourist tax.
  • Cancellation terms. What happens in case of force majeure, bad weather or cancelled flights?
We take full charge of the legal side: we check every clause of the contract, guarantee there are no hidden commissions and help arrange deposit insurance. This way you insure your deposit and don't worry about minor damage during manoeuvres.

Stage 4. Who will handle the boat? The captain and crew

The format of managing a charter depends on your experience and preferred style of holiday. We cover every possible scenario.

Scenario A. You are an experienced licensed captain (bareboat)

If you hold international licences (RYA, IYT, ICC) and a radio-operator certificate (VHF) — the helm is yours.

How we help: we prepare the boat, check the documents and do a preliminary check-in, so you don't spend hours inspecting every pump, and we stay in touch around the clock for any technical questions in the sailing area.

Scenario B. A novice captain or new waters (support / flotilla)

If you have a licence but lack the practice for tricky mooring in narrow marinas or passages through unfamiliar straits.

How we help: we arrange for your boat to join a flotilla led by our experienced flagship skipper. You sail your own boat with your friends, but the lead captain is always nearby: he'll advise on the forecast, help you berth in the marina and stay in touch by radio. If needed, we provide a navigator-instructor for the first few days.

Scenario C. Full relaxation without a licence (skippered)

You simply want to rest with your family or company without getting into navigation.

How we help: we provide a qualified English-speaking captain who is responsible for safety and knows the best secret restaurants, deserted beaches and turquoise caves. On request we expand the crew with a chef (dishes from local seafood) and a steward/hostess.

Stage 5. Logistics, provisioning and "onshore" service

The worst thing after an exhausting flight is to spend half a day of your precious holiday on a trip to the supermarket. Buying drinking water, heavy bags of groceries and household chemicals and hauling them aboard in the heat is exactly what kills the sense of celebration.

Beyond that, there remain questions:

  • booking berths in marinas (in high season in Croatia or on Capri, spots need to be reserved weeks in advance);
  • transfers from the airport straight to the pier;
  • hire of extra gear (SUP boards, seabobs, snorkelling or diving equipment).
We deliver a "turnkey provisioning" concept. The day before your arrival we fill the catamaran's fridges and lockers to your personal wishlist. You step aboard — and chilled prosecco, fresh fruit, your favourite cheeses and a SUP board ready on deck are already waiting. We also arrange a comfortable transfer and book berths in the best marinas along your route.

In summary: the sea voyage as it should be

Chartering a yacht or catamaran is not a short-term hire of transport. It's an investment in your emotions, memories and personal comfort. And 90% of the impression of the whole voyage depends on how well every organisational stage is worked out.

With Serenity Yachting Club you don't need to become an expert in maritime law, negotiate with foreign charterers or worry about security deposits. Your task is to choose a destination, gather your closest people and bring the sunscreen. Everything else — from choosing the ideal catamaran anywhere in the world to freshly brewed coffee as the sails go up — we take on ourselves.
